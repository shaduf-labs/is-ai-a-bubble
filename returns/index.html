Is AI a Bubble?

Investment returns

Useful output must earn back capital. Investors must also earn back the price they paid for ownership.

Financial and offering evidence: 18 September 2026. Demand and receipt correction: 19 September 2026. Retrieval-day boundaries, not publication dates or complete same-day balances.

Capital recoveryKey sensitivitiesOwnership pricesOffering proceeds

A profitable business is not automatically a good investment at any cost or price. Recovery depends on the amount and timing of cash after operating and replacement needs. An ownership valuation adds another hurdle: what future success was already paid for?

Start with an investment’s earning life

A gross margin deducts some direct costs. Operating profit includes more expenses and accounting depreciation. Operating cash flow adjusts noncash items and working capital but does not deduct all investment. None alone tells us whether a particular investment has recovered its capital and earned a return for the risk taken.

The whole-system assessment uses $14.117bn—CoreWeave’s disclosed first-half cash-investment scale—to illustrate an investment cohort. It does not assert that all of that mixed spending entered service together or that it describes a new GPU-only fleet. The public evidence does not supply the cohort-specific prices, utilization, maintenance and asset mix needed to underwrite the actual investment. Disclosed investment input.

At four operating years and an assumed 50% complete pre-financing cash margin, the required level annual revenue is $8.907bn. Approximately $4.454bn of annual cash then has a present value equal to the $14.117bn investment at a 10% hurdle. This is a conditional break-even calculation including return on capital, not simply cost divided by years.

The margin means cash after operating costs, tax and maintenance, before financing payments. It is not the reported EBITDA margin. Interest is not deducted a second time when the discount rate already represents the all-capital hurdle. New investment needed after the chosen earning life is not supplied free by the model.

The demand study adds a cost-sensitive test. With revenue normalized to 100, assumed variable cash costs of 35 and fixed costs of 15, cash is 50. A 20% price cut and 25% volume increase restore revenue to 100, but variable costs rise to 43.75 and cash falls to 41.25. At the inherited four-year investment scale, the scenario has a −$2.470bn net present value. It is not an actual project loss.

A 20% fall in unit variable costs restores that cash. Without the cost improvement, the assumed structure needs 44.4% more volume—not 25%—to restore original cash. Extra work must fit available capacity; expansion capital is not included. The stress is not calibrated to a mix-sensitive token-price series. See the revenue-versus-cash visual and complete sensitivity table.

Cheaper service can help buyers without rescuing every supplier

Lower unit costs can make more useful tasks worth buying. Competition can also pass savings to customers, and customers can change their model mix or supplier without stopping AI use. The crucial conjunction is adequate paid volume, attainable prices, controlled all-in costs and sufficient duration. More use by itself is not enough.

The office buyer example shows that a moderate license cost can be justified by a fraction of saved-time value. The support example shows that a billable outcome need not eliminate an equivalent human expense. Good customer economics support willingness to pay; they do not allocate every dollar of customer surplus to the model or infrastructure provider. Office buyer hurdle; support buyer hurdle.

Power and delivery matter to that duration. The IEA’s April 2026 outlook estimated global data-center electricity use at 485 TWh in 2025 and projected 950 TWh in 2030. A forecast of energy demand is not delivered capacity or cash available for a particular project. Grid and construction constraints can restrain excess capacity while also delaying receipts. IEA scope and forecast.

An ownership price asks a different cash question

A share buyer does not buy one year’s profit. The price reflects a claim on future distributions and realizable ownership value, affected by reinvestment, financing and dilution. High current earnings refute a claim that nothing commercial is happening; they do not justify an arbitrary price.

The whole-system assessment’s Microsoft exercise uses a timestamped 17 September 2026 observation, 19:29:50 UTC, at $496.03 and about $3.692trn equity value. That observation lacked a durable vendor page and a same-instant share-count reconciliation. It is retained only as an illustrative input, not presented here as a verified current quotation or a fair-value estimate. Price provenance and full model.

Its annual equity-cash proxy is $63.886bn: operating cash less cash property/equipment purchases and finance-lease principal. Allowing for the full $12.405bn stock-compensation expense lowers the proxy to $51.481bn. That allowance is a rough treatment of dilution, not measured repurchase cash. At the middle assumptions, the first path reaches roughly $448bn annual cash in year ten, and about 69% of present value comes from later cash. Terminal assumptions therefore matter greatly. The exercise does not precisely model nonoperating assets, future borrowing, acquisitions, taxes or dilution; recognizing some omitted assets consistently could lower the operating-growth requirement. Cash growth is not necessarily revenue growth. The model neither supplies a trading target nor establishes impossibility.

A financing-round value is not money already earned

The dated March OpenAI post-money value of $852bn and May Anthropic value of $965bn price ownership at those rounds. They are not the cash raised, enterprise values or audited sales. The May round includes $15bn of earlier hyperscaler commitments, so those commitments cannot be added again. Later reported revenue run rates do not silently replace a dated denominator. OpenAI round; Anthropic round.

One explicit scenario assumes no owner distributions for ten years, followed by cash growing at 3% indefinitely, discounted at 10%. It needs $154.7bn of OpenAI owner cash in year eleven, or $175.2bn for Anthropic. At an assumed 25% complete owner-cash margin, those correspond to $618.8bn and $700.8bn of revenue. The margin and timing are not observed lab economics. Private sensitivity and equations.

Earlier distributions would lower the endpoint requirement. Additional cash needs or new equity can raise an existing owner’s hurdle. Favorable reporting that Anthropic expected a second consecutive adjusted operating-profit quarter, excluding stock compensation, is relevant counterevidence to a perpetual-loss story. The full investor documents were unavailable and the article was paywalled; adjusted operating profit is not audited distributable cash after all reinvestment. Attributed profitability report.

An offering changes the financing—not the asset’s economics

The evidence inspected through 18 September distinguished company-announced confidential draft submissions, public exhibits, reported preparations and completed financing. It did not establish publicly priced OpenAI or Anthropic offering terms. A reported timetable is not a successful listing or verified primary proceeds. CoreWeave was already public; its proposed September financing was not an initial offering. Company-by-company dated stage record.

A listing can broaden funding access and reveal financial information. It cannot make an uneconomic project earn its cost simply by changing owners. A postponed offering is also not automatically insolvency: the question is available cash and obligations without assuming proceeds from an unclosed transaction.

What would make the return case stronger?

The decisive evidence is realized cash from identifiable delivered investments: service prices and paid utilization, complete operating costs, maintenance and replacement needs, productive life and actual amortization. A backlog helps only to the extent delivery occurs and the counterparty can pay. Investor pricing then needs a separate test against that cash and dilution.

Customer demand makes that outcome plausible in important settings. It has not established the assumed 50% cash margin or a four- to six-year cash stream for the investment cohort. Evidence that would strengthen or weaken the answer →
